# Candidate lines (drafting notes)

Written against the judge's published brief, in order: a small good thing
that happened today, warmth, surprise, one concrete detail, fresh wording
(no greeting-card phrasing).

1. At 7:12 this morning my neighbour knocked with two persimmons and said,
   "The tree made too many again" — the tree is one branch over a fence.
2. The vending machine gave me two cans by mistake today, and the stranger
   behind me, who got the second, tapped it against mine like a toast.
3. Today my dad, who never texts, sent his first one: no words, just a photo
   of the first strawberry from his balcony pot, the size of a pea, and he
   was so proud.
4. At lunch the cafeteria lady slipped an extra croquette onto my tray and
   whispered, "That one was lopsided, it needs a home."
5. A kid on the train traded me his window seat for my aisle seat today,
   then spent the whole ride pressed to the glass whispering cloud names.
6. My umbrella flipped inside out in the rain today, and a passing
   grandmother laughed, fixed it, and said, "It just wanted to see the sky."
7. A toddler held the elevator button with both hands today, tongue out in
   concentration, for a man carrying three watermelons.
8. At 6 a.m. the baker gave me yesterday's melon pan free because, he said,
   it had "dreamed too long" — it was still warm from today's oven.
9. My cat brought me a leaf today and dropped it on my keyboard like rent:
   not a bug this time, just the reddest maple leaf on the street.
10. The lost-dog poster on our street got a handwritten update today:
    "FOUND — he came home smelling of someone else's barbecue."
11. A stranger in the supermarket reached the top-shelf tea for me today,
    then asked me to reach the bottom-shelf biscuits for him — we both
    left taller.

## Pick

Candidate 3 — the dad / strawberry line. It is the warmest (a silent dad
breaking a lifetime habit for something tiny), it has the concrete detail
the brief asks for (a strawberry the size of a pea, a balcony pot, a text
with no words), and it is told in plain, un-borrowed words. Refinement
pass: give the ending a turn — his pride should be measured against
something absurdly larger than a pea, so the smile lands at the last word.
